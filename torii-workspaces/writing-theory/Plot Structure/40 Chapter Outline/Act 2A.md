# Act 2A

First half of the middle section, showing a positive rising action as the protagonist enters new world, learns grows and has more success than failure. 



**11. First Plot Point** (decision to go for adventure)  
Protagonist actively choses to enter new world and its' comfortable (it can literally be new environment, but also something like change of setting, eg. trying something new). This is a big turning point in the story. There's no going back at this point in the story. The protagonist still has flaws, but is trying to fight the challenges of the new world.



**12. Enemies &amp; Allies** (introduce cast remains)  
We get a sense of the new life in the new world as the protagonist feels like a fish out of water and their new role is established. Any remaining characters should be introduced here, as well as character archetypes like love interest or ally, at least one of them representing the B story. Often there can be conflicts with these characters from whome protagonist faces antagonism.



**13. The B Story** (working on the flaw)  
Protagonist spends time with B story character, where the B story character figuratively holds mirror to protagonist (e.g. Zuko to Zhao, Aang to Zuko, Strider to Nazgul, Frodo to Gollum). Explained why protagonist can't go back and given reasons to continue. (e.g. Frodo learning of what could happen in Galadriels' mirror). The theme is again restated here, but protagonist yet refuses to learn (e.g. Iroh repeatedly offering Zuko "calming" theme, to which he "angrily without thinking" responds)



**14. Games &amp; Trials**  
Some of the more interesting things the new world offers are presented. The protagonist explores the new world and learns to navigate it, they receive some form of training and grow from it (e.g. as a form of a challenge, where even losing makes the protagonist understand new things = grow)
